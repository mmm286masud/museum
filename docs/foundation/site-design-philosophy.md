# Site Design Philosophy

## Core Claim

The museum should feel like a guided exhibit, not a generic landing page and
not a retro gaming fan blog.

The design works when visitors feel they are moving through labeled rooms and
placards with a curator guiding their attention.

## Governing Metaphor

This site behaves like a museum entrance hall that opens into a sequence of
artifact rooms.

That metaphor controls:

- layout rhythm
- copy tone
- labeling
- navigation
- image treatment

## Why The Visual Language Is Swiss

The museum uses Swiss design because it supports:

- strong hierarchy
- grid-based order
- controlled spacing
- typographic discipline
- visual restraint

That restraint matters. The devices should feel historically significant, not
buried under nostalgic decoration.

## Explorer Archetype

The tone should invite discovery.

The visitor should feel that the museum is saying:

- here is what this object meant
- here is why it survives in memory
- here is where the next room leads

Curiosity is the emotional engine. Hype is not.

## Scarcity Principle

Scarcity belongs in this site as curatorial context.

Good scarcity language:

- surviving intact examples are harder to find
- packaging, screens, and battery contacts degrade over time
- preservation changes how we read the artifact now

Bad scarcity language:

- sales urgency
- collector flex language
- ecommerce-style pressure

## What Gives The Layout Its Order

The site should keep repeating a stable grammar:

1. orient the visitor
2. introduce the room
3. spotlight one device at a time
4. transition to the next historical shift

Readers should learn the structure quickly so they can focus on the exhibit,
not on decoding new page patterns every section.

## How The Curator Thinks About Evidence

Every durable exhibit package should include:

- device title
- historical context
- key features
- cultural impact
- visual artifact
- short narrative placard

That combination turns an object into an exhibit rather than a product card.

## Images

Images should do one of three jobs:

1. show the artifact clearly
2. support historical atmosphere
3. explain a design or cultural idea

If an image does none of those jobs, it is probably noise.

## What To Carry Into Later Rooms

Future pages should preserve:

- the guided route
- the typographic seriousness
- the museum tone
- the clear separation between main route and supporting material

Future pages should not copy one exact composition forever. They should keep
the same logic while adapting the emphasis to the artifact in that room.
