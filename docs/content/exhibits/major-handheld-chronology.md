# Major Handheld Chronology

## Purpose

This document records the approved major-device sequence for the chronology hall
on the homepage.

## Room 01 — Origins

### Nintendo Game Boy (1989)

- headline: Portable, defined.
- subheadline: Discover the gray artifact that turned gaming into a journey you could carry.
- key highlight: Long battery life made handheld play truly everyday.
- minimal description: Its monochrome screen and durable body made portability practical, not experimental. This is the object that moved games from the living room into daily life.
- scarcity line: Clean screens, intact battery contacts, and preserved early cartridges are increasingly harder to discover in the archive.

## Room 02 — Color Evolution

### Game Boy Color (1998)

- headline: Color, carried.
- subheadline: Explore the moment handheld worlds stepped out of green haze and into a brighter journey.
- key highlight: Full color refreshed a classic form without losing portability.
- minimal description: It kept the Game Boy line familiar while making game worlds feel more immediate and alive. This room marks the shift from endurance alone to visual expression.
- scarcity line: Fading translucent shells, battery corrosion, and disappearing boxed units make strong survivors rarer every year.

## Room 03 — Performance Expansion

### Game Boy Advance (2001)

- headline: Power, widened.
- subheadline: Continue the journey into a slimmer handheld built for broader ambition.
- key highlight: 32-bit performance brought console-scale design into the palm.
- minimal description: The horizontal form and stronger hardware expanded what portable games could look and feel like. It stands as the point where handheld design became sharper, faster, and more assured.
- scarcity line: Bright original screens, uncracked shells, and preserved cartridge libraries are harder to explore intact now.

## Room 04 — Multimedia Era

### PlayStation Portable (2004 Japan launch)

- headline: Media, in motion.
- subheadline: Discover a handheld that invited visitors to explore games, music, and film through one polished object.
- key highlight: A widescreen display made handheld entertainment feel cinematic.
- minimal description: The PSP pushed portability toward multimedia luxury, with a form closer to premium electronics than toys. In museum terms, it captures the moment handhelds chased a broader media identity.
- scarcity line: Scratched screens, fragile disc drives, and vanishing UMD media make well-kept systems increasingly scarce.

## Room 05 — Innovation Era

### Nintendo DS (2004)

- headline: Touch changes everything.
- subheadline: Explore the handheld that split the screen and reopened the journey through play.
- key highlight: Dual screens and touch control changed how portable games were imagined.
- minimal description: The DS broke the familiar handheld template and made interaction itself the exhibit. It opened a new design path through touch, asymmetry, and experimentation.
- scarcity line: Intact hinges, original styluses, and preserved dual-screen hardware are steadily disappearing from the archive.

### Nintendo 3DS (2011)

- headline: Depth, revealed.
- subheadline: Journey into a device that asked players to discover portable space without glasses.
- key highlight: Glasses-free 3D made depth part of the handheld experience.
- minimal description: The 3DS extended Nintendo's experimental line with spatial display, cameras, and a stronger digital identity. It represents a bold attempt to make handheld play feel dimensional and new again.
- scarcity line: Aging batteries, worn circle pads, and delisted digital history make complete surviving systems more limited over time.

## Room 06 — Modern Era

### Nintendo Switch (2017)

- headline: One system. Anywhere.
- subheadline: Explore the device that turned the journey between handheld and home into one continuous motion.
- key highlight: Dock-to-hand play redefined what portability could mean.
- minimal description: The Switch collapsed the old divide between portable system and living-room console. As an exhibit, it marks the modern moment when flexibility became the central design idea.
- scarcity line: Early launch units, intact Joy-Con hardware, and limited physical editions are already becoming the harder pieces of the modern archive.
